Two home bases, several options each

Accommodation

All of these are walkable to old-town life. The ones marked "in this itinerary" are what the Day-by-Day plan assumes — swap in any alternative without changing the rest of the trip.

Hotel Colors Inn

Nights 3–6 · Mostar / Blagaj

Hotel Kriva Ćuprija

In this itineraryCentral Mostar

Riverside, right by the Old Bridge, simple rooms with a terrace over the Radobolja stream. Very easy, very central — no taxi needed for evenings out.

Muslibegović House

Alternative in this itineraryHistoric

A restored 17th-century Ottoman residence turned small hotel, part-museum, full of carved wood and courtyard gardens. Sleep inside the history rather than beside it.

Hotel Buna

In BlagajRiverside

A proper hotel right at the Buna spring in Blagaj, known for its trout dinners on the water. Puts you a short walk from the Tekija instead of a bus ride — trades Mostar's evening buzz for quiet and a view.

Zen Zone Blagaj

House / villaIndependent

A private house right on the Buna river in Blagaj with its own spring-fed access and a pool, full kitchen. More self-catered and independent than a hotel — good if you want a quieter, more nature-forward base and don't mind a short taxi into Mostar for dinners.

One decision to make

Getting Around

Public transport handles Sarajevo and the train down to Mostar easily. The only real question is whether to add a rental car for the Herzegovina half.

Option A

Stay car-free

  • Train Sarajevo ↔ Mostar (scenic, no driving to think about)
  • Blagaj by local bus or short taxi
  • Kravice + Počitelj as a guided day tour
  • Vineyard tasting via pickup from Mostar
  • Simplest option — everything pre-arranged, nothing to park
Option B

Rent a car in Mostar only

  • Still take the train Sarajevo → Mostar — no need to drive that leg or deal with old-town parking
  • Pick up the car on arrival in Mostar (Day 3), drop off before the return train (Day 7)
  • Kravice, Blagaj, Počitelj and the vineyard all become 20–45 min self-timed drives
  • Opens up a Dubrovnik day trip on the coast (see below) without adding a night
  • Trade-off: one border crossing by car if you go to Croatia, and you do need to find parking in Mostar itself (a short walk from the old town, not inside it)
The ocean question

Neum or Dubrovnik

Bosnia has 20km of coastline at Neum; Croatia's Dubrovnik is further but far more scenic. Both are realistic add-ons if you rent the car in Mostar.

~1.5hrs from Mostar · no border

Neum

Bosnia's only coastal town — modest pebble beaches, relaxed seafood lunches, no passport needed. Fine for an afternoon, not a highlight of the trip.

~2.5hrs from Mostar · Croatia border

Dubrovnik

Old town walls, turquoise Adriatic, a genuinely beautiful coast road along the way. With a car this fits as a long day trip instead of needing an overnight. Bring passports.

    Practical notes

    • Currency is the convertible mark (BAM) — carry small notes, bus counters and drivers are usually cash-only.
    • Buy Sarajevo–Mostar train/bus tickets same-day at the station; no need to book ahead outside festival weeks.
    • Check the train timetable the day before — it doesn't run every day year-round, so have the bus as a backup.
    • Book the Kravice day tour and vineyard tasting a day or two ahead in peak season (July–August, less needed in June/September).
    • Comfortable shoes throughout — cobblestones in both old towns, uneven paths at Blagaj and Kravice.
    Built for a week of good coffee, better wine, and no time spent looking for parking.

    One decision to make

    Getting Around

    Cluj and Sighișoara connect easily by train or bus. The question is whether a rental car is worth it for reaching the Saxon villages and the vineyard on your own schedule.

    Option A

    Stay car-free

    • Bus Cluj ↔ Sighișoara (~2h15m, several daily) or the slower train (~3h, scenic)
    • Cheile Turzii by minibus/maxitaxi from Cluj (~40 min to Turda, then a short local hop)
    • Biertan + Viscri as a guided day tour — the direct public transport between Saxon villages is thin
    • Jidvei winery tasting via a pre-booked transfer or small-group tour from Sighișoara
    • Simplest option — no parking to think about in either old town
    Option B

    Rent a car in Sighișoara only

    • Still take the bus or train Cluj → Sighișoara — skip driving that leg or parking in Cluj's center
    • Pick up the car on arrival in Sighișoara (Day 3), drop off before heading back to Cluj (Day 7)
    • Biertan, Viscri, and Jidvei all become flexible 30–50 min drives, at your own pace
    • Opens the door to a detour toward Sibiu or the Făgăraș foothills if you want a bigger hike
    • Trade-off: village roads are narrow and occasionally unpaved near Viscri — nothing serious, just slow down

    Activities

    Limestone canyon hike

    Citadel & a proper foodie day

    Saxon fortified villages

    A guided minibus day (or your rental car) to Biertan's UNESCO-listed fortified church, then on to Viscri — a preserved Saxon village with a working blacksmith and a guesthouse King Charles III still visits. Slow, rural, genuinely quiet.

      Practical notes

      • Currency is the Romanian leu (RON) — useful to carry cash in the Saxon villages, where cards aren't always accepted.
      • Buy Cluj–Sighișoara bus or train tickets a day ahead or same-day; the bus is faster (~2h15m) than the train (~3h) on this route.
      • Book the Biertan/Viscri day tour and the vineyard tasting a few days ahead — both run smaller groups than the Bosnia equivalents.
      • Romanian is a Romance language — if either of you speaks French, Italian, or Spanish, menus and signs will be easier than expected.
      • Comfortable shoes throughout — cobblestones in both old towns, uneven terrain on the Cheile Turzii trail.
